#### Goals for this Module
- Get the last of my materials and finish making the physical prototype of my game Sightline

- Present my findings to the class to see what they think and see where there is room for adjustment

#### Progress
- **What I accomplished**:
  - I put together the physical prototype for my game over spring break, it took a whole day
  - Its satisfying to hold and is self contained, so I can store all of its assets within the board itself
  - It is composed mostly of cardboard and big blue tape used for painting walls
  - It features two boards since it is asymmetrical in natural, die, playing pieces and a deck of cards
  - To reiterate, the phantom gets map wide knowledge on positions and has to decide where to go to cut them off
  - The investigator has to hunt for anchors in the darkness to cleanse and play around the phantom
- **Challenges faced**:
  - I ended up testing the game out with a friend and it did not play out the way I imagined
  - The game was sluggish and took a great amount of time to get the instructions down
  - The problem exactly was that I was playing the role of both the phantom and computer, so I had to do both tasks
  - And with only one person to go for, there were no decisions to make, I just went straight for them
- **Solutions**:
  - As we played, we decided to change the rules to make it a more fun experience
  - This involved making some mechanics tied to cards, base games mechanics
  - It is possible I was relying on them too much to solve the games core issues
  - The speed of both players was doubled to make getting around the playing space quicker
  - We also tried to take away some of responsibilities the computer would handle by asking each other specified question outloud
  - This made things feel a lot more fluid to play and is the experience I was hoping for
    
#### Learnings
- Making a game is hard, and nothing played out how I thought it would
- We had to make up things on the spot, but having a physical model meant I could adjust and try them out immediately
- The physical board did not change, but the way we played around it did and that made all the difference
- The biggest change was limiting the phantom, this made it so I was making more decisions instead of being all knowing
- I believe it made it so there was more give and take, interactions happening between both players in a more dynamic manner

#### Free Thinking
- I multiplied the roll of our die by two to make moving quicker and easier
- Every turn the phantom could pick a room to see if the investigator was in it and could no longer peak at their board
- Every turn the investigator could ask to reveal if an anchor was placed in a room or not
- My friend got the god card to reveal all anchors halfway through the match
- Since we both knew where the other wanted to be, it made it this interesting guessing game between players
- Cards that only last a set amount of turns should just last the entire match because the game's length does not support them
- To give the investigator more distance away from a zone, they get to play again after cleansing an anchor
- Maybe the god card should be made base hit once two out of the five anchors are cleansed to make the last one more interesting
- Possibly show anchor locations to investigators at the start of the match, but not the routes to get to them
- Maybe make it so it does not take a round to cleanse anchors, it could just be a pick up
